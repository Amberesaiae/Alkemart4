# 01 — Market position and principles

## Strategic synthesis

Alkemart should not clone a single incumbent.

| Reference | Strength to adopt | Constraint to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Jiji | Broad seller participation, location, condition, directness | Listing duplication, weak normalized comparison, limited commerce operations |
| Jumia | Retail taxonomy, structured facets, campaigns, checkout, official-store trust | Promotion overload, generic marketplace feel, seller identity becoming secondary |
| Hubtel | Nearby stores, Ghanaian payment behavior, delivery confidence | Store-first discovery that makes cross-store comparison difficult |
| Mowafer concept | Product hub and explicit price comparison | External scraping and context not native to Ghanaian multi-seller fulfillment |

Alkemart's defendable combination is:

```text
structured retail discovery
+ seller-owned storefronts
+ exact-product offer comparison
+ Ghana-native payment and fulfillment
+ marketplace trust and governance
```

## Positioning category

Use **structured multi-vendor retail marketplace** externally and
**structured community commerce** internally.

“Community” describes merchant ownership and durable buyer-shop relationships.
It does not mean informal visual treatment, public bargaining, social-feed
clutter, or reduced catalog quality.

## Customer groups

### Buyers

- Mobile-first Ghanaian shoppers comparing price and availability.
- Buyers who know the desired product or brand but not the best seller.
- Buyers who want credible local shops, MoMo/COD, pickup, and delivery clarity.
- Category explorers who need guided specifications rather than endless cards.

### Sellers

- Professional retailers and distributors.
- Independent boutiques and specialist shops.
- Growing product brands with their own stock.
- Regional merchants moving from WhatsApp/Instagram selling into managed
  commerce.

## Experience principles

### Product-first, shop-visible

Category and search journeys lead with products. The selected offer always
names the shop. Store journeys lead with shop identity and collections.

### Progressive structure

The platform requests the minimum information needed to publish honestly, then
guides the seller toward richer identifiers, attributes, media, inventory, and
policies. Structure is a growth path, not an arbitrary admission barrier.

### Exact comparison is earned

Two offers appear as seller alternatives only when they refer to the same
canonical product and variant. Similar products are recommendations, not peer
offers.

### Total offer, not headline price

Comparison includes item price, delivery cost, availability, ETA/pickup,
condition, return protection, and seller reliability. The ranking rationale
must be explainable.

### Trust comes from evidence

Verification labels identify exactly what was checked. Operational performance
and verified-purchase outcomes matter more than decorative badges.

### Classy restraint

Department colour supports wayfinding. Campaign art tells the story. Product
cards stay neutral and merchandise-led. Typography, spacing, and imagery create
the premium standard; adding more labels does not.

### Ghana-native commerce

MoMo, COD, pickup, landmark/digital address support, regional delivery, and
intermittent stock/connection realities are core product requirements.

## Marketplace boundaries

The launch catalog covers shippable or collectable goods. Property, jobs, CVs,
and open-ended services must not be forced into Product → Variant → Offer. If
introduced, they require separate vertical models and journeys.

## Success definition

The strategy is working when:

- buyers can find a product without knowing a seller;
- buyers can understand why two prices differ;
- a seller owns a credible, shareable shop and can operate it daily;
- catalog quality improves without excluding merchants lacking formal product
  identifiers;
- campaigns increase demand without corrupting taxonomy or trust;
- search, category, product, and store pages are useful to people and crawlable
  without producing duplicate content;
- marketplace decisions are driven by behavior and commercial outcomes rather
  than visual imitation.
